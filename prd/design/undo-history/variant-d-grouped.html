Undo history — Variant D · Grouped for density

Thirty entries is the cap and the opening state. The list groups itself into work bursts — runs of activity separated by an idle gap — so the whole history fits without a scrollbar. Hover any row or group header to see the span it reverses; the rail down the left gutter draws the contiguous rule as one unbroken line from the top of the list to your cursor.

Why bursts, not clock buckets

A fixed bucket (“14:0x”) cuts wherever the clock happens to roll, which lands mid-sweep as often as not. This groups on the idle gap instead: a new group starts when more than 8 minutes passed since the previous action, or when a group reaches 8 entries. Cuts land where the analyst actually stopped and thought.

The header is identified by its time span, never by a derived title. Titles like “Mixed work” carry no information and go stale the moment the group changes.

What opens expanded

The newest group only. Almost every undo is “the last thing, or the last two things”; those must be one click away with no disclosure step in between. Deep undo is a decision, not a reflex, and one extra click is the right friction for it.

It also means 30 entries open as about 9 rows of chrome — the whole history visible at once, no scrollbar. Expand all shows the wall this is saving you from.

Grouping can only coarsen

A collapsed group is taken whole or not at all. You can never reach into the middle of one without expanding it first, so collapsing makes the grain coarser and never opens a route to undoing out of order.

The rail runs unbroken from the top of the list to the cursor, past collapsed groups and through sealed rows alike, and the dashed cut line separates reversed from kept. Everything above the cut goes, always.

Sealed items are pass-through

Sealed rows carry a Saved tag at all times. A collapsed group holding one says “1 saved” on its summary line before you touch it, and its header note reads “6 of 7” rather than “all 7”.

Inside a hovered span a sealed row takes no accent tint and is struck through, while the rail passes straight through it — skipped, not a wall. After the undo it survives, alone at the top of the list.

Below 9 entries, the groups go away

Grouping earns nothing on a short history and costs a header row per group. Under the threshold the menu renders flat with no group chrome at all — press 5 in the prototype bar to see it. Same rail, same cut line, same footer.

Two runs are never one row

Every pivot row carries a global run number for its provider, the origin it was run from, and its own node/edge counts and timestamp. Four AIL runs read as four different lines even when collapsed into the same group.

All counts in this prototype are computed from the mock graph, not written by hand — the footer numbers are the real diff of the state the click would produce.
