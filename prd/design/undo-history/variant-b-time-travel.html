Undo history · variant B

Time travel

One menu, not two. Undo and Redo open the same list; a now line runs through it, with completed operations above and undone ones below. Clicking travels to that point — above the line it undoes, below it redoes. The list is chronological, oldest at the top, so distance from the line is distance from the present in both directions.

Prototype controls Menu open: ↑ ↓ to aim, Enter to travel, Esc to close. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y step one.
Why oldest at the top The brief fixes completed operations above the line and undone ones below. That only holds in chronological order: the row touching the line from above is the next undo, the row touching it from below is the next redo, and moving away from the line in either direction moves away from the present. Newest-first would put the redo stack above everything.
Hover reads as the after-state An undo span drains to grey and picks up a strike; a redo span fills back in from grey to full ink. The arrow gutter and the dashed ghost NOW line say where the line lands. On the canvas, nodes about to leave go dashed and faint, nodes about to return draw as dashed outlines.
Sealed rows stay applied A sealed row inside a span is skipped, so after the travel it sits below the line while still being applied. It keeps full ink, a hatched preview fill and a SAVED chip rather than the grey of a genuinely undone row — the state rule 3 creates, shown rather than hidden.
Opening from Redo The Redo caret sets data-side="redo": the accent rail moves to the lower half, the header chip flips, the list scrolls the now-line near the top so the redo stack fills the view, and the first redo row is pre-armed with its span already previewed. The click that follows is in the same place it would have been in a redo-only menu.
