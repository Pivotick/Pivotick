Fifty providers in a 420 pixel panel

A live misp-modules service advertises 118 enrichment modules. Register one pivot per module and a single IP address puts 50 of them in the pivot panel at once — a flat, unsearchable list nine times taller than the space it is given. Five ways to fix it, each built here as a real panel at real size, running on the real catalogue.

What each one costs

The five are not variants of one idea. A and B keep the panel as it is and make the list navigable. C changes what the list is about. D changes what a run is. E moves the list off the panel entirely. They combine: A is the floor, and C stacks cleanly on top of B or D.

A+CThe cheap, honest pair. A filter box is a contained change to PivotPanel. Availability needs one optional field on PivotDefinition and is the only thing here that stops an analyst running a provider that cannot answer.
DRetires the 1061px facet. The Enrich (all applicable) meta-pivot exists because there was no way to run several providers at once. If the list itself is the selection, the meta-pivot and its fifty-checkbox facet both go away.
EThe most change for the most headroom. A palette stops the panel caring how long the catalogue is — but it hides browsing, and browsing is how an analyst learns what a new deployment can do.
cutGrouping does not work here, and the catalogue says so. Group by what a module accepts and 46 of the 50 IP providers land in one bucket; by what it does, 32 are called Lookup; by what it returns, 50 of 118 declare nothing at all. Fifty ways to look an address up do not sort into six drawers, so no design above tries.

Every panel above is interactive. Type in A and E, open a group in B, unlock C, tick rows in D, and switch the origin to watch each design at 50 providers, at 11, and at 1.
